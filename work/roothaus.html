Case study 08 / E-commerce, client

RootHaus

A heritage leather goods storefront selling across seven currencies to a global, Nigerian-heritage diaspora audience, built so the brand's craft story lives inside the shopping experience instead of an About page nobody reads before checking out.

RoleFull-stack build, brand support
StackMulti-currency commerce, cart, restock requests
TypeClient engagement
StatusView live product ↗
01 / The problem

Luxury leather brands live or die on the story behind the material, the tannery, the craft, the permanence of a well-made object, and most small leather goods sellers end up on a generic commerce template that quietly undercuts exactly the premium positioning they're trying to build. The template puts the product grid first and exiles the brand story to an About page that almost nobody reads before deciding whether to buy, which means the thing that's supposed to justify a premium price never actually reaches the customer at the moment it matters.

02 / The thinking

I rejected the standard product-grid homepage with the story tucked away elsewhere. The heritage narrative, rooted in the Iroko tree and the idea of permanence and craft, is built directly into the shopping experience itself, so a browsing customer encounters the "why" before or alongside the "what," not after they've already decided to leave.

I also rejected letting discontinued pieces simply vanish from the catalog the way most stores handle sellouts. An archive request flow lets a customer register interest in a piece that's no longer listed, which turns a dead product page into an ongoing signal of demand instead of a quiet loss, and gives the brand real data on what's worth remaking.

03 / The business and the users

The audience is genuinely global: a Nigerian-heritage diaspora spread across markets that don't share a currency, which meant currency switching had to be immediate and visible on every page, not buried two clicks deep in a settings menu the way most storefronts treat it as an afterthought.

The catalog itself spans four distinct categories, men, women, pets, and accessories and homewares, and the site had to hold one coherent visual system across all four without fragmenting into four different-feeling stores, which is a harder design constraint than it sounds once product photography, tone, and layout all have to flex per category while staying recognizably one brand.

04 / The outcome

Live, taking orders across multiple currencies with a working cart and the archive request pipeline already collecting real interest data on discontinued pieces. The brand story is no longer competing with the checkout flow for attention, it's part of it, which is the specific problem this build set out to solve.
